YourKhata Shop · Design direction · revision 3 · Saturday 17 Oct 2026

Counter Mode, corrected: right numbers first, then a calmer, faster screen.

This revision answers the outside review point by point. Every screen now uses one example shop whose totals add up, every flow says what really happens in the product today, and anything that would need a new rule or a change to a shared component is marked. It is still design only: no code, data or deployment was touched.

Step 1

Where I agree with the review, and where I don't

The review scored the last version about 7.4 out of 10 from screenshots. Most of its points are right, and three found real mistakes in my data. A few needed amending once I checked them against the code.

Agree
₹12,40,000 beside a ₹18,450 total was wrong. It was meant as a width test but sat in real data. The example shop now has all 12 customers listed, and they add up to ₹18,450. Large amounts are tested separately and labelled as a stress test.
Agree
17 Oct 2026 is a Saturday. Every date and comparison now says Saturday, and "last Saturday" is 10 Oct.
Agree
The hourly chart ran to 7 pm at 4:40 pm. It now stops at the current hour, names its metric ("billed, with GST, after returns") and its range, and the comparison is a sentence, not a hidden line.
Agree
The void example mixed up a return and a cancellation. Bill actions now start with "What happened?" and lead to four separate flows. The void dialog lists what the code actually does: stock comes back, the bill leaves the dues, any payment stays with the customer as an advance, and a bill with a credit note can't be voided.
Agree
Tiny register marks, the vague "Start" tab and the "People" tab. Rows now name their type in words, status is a word pill, and the phone bar reads Home · Bills · Customers · Suppliers · More with drawn icons.
Agree
Hindi lacked the tabs and actions. The Hindi phone now has the same parts as English, in the same order.
Amend
"Don't claim the touch area solves accessibility." Fair, and the claim is narrower now. The 44 × 44 area is real (.ub-hit in app/globals.css) and the axe sweep checks it at 390 px (DESIGN-SYSTEM §5). What's still unproven is overlap between neighbours, which needs 12 px between them on a phone.
Amend
"Show allocation errors." The current payment form can't produce a mismatch: "Auto, oldest first" is on by default, and manual amounts are capped at each bill's due, with the rest shown as "kept as advance" (PaymentAllocationPicker.tsx). The real cases to design for are an advance, and a bill that was paid on another device while the form was open. Both are shown.
Amend
"Clarify what the receipt share sends." The server's receipt text already has a "Receipt: link" line to the public receipt page (payments/services/receipt.py). So the share is text with a link, and the sheet now says so. The words that left are not recorded yet (message inventory M-03).
Amend
"Paid ahead" label in filters. The list's real balance filter has three values: owes you, you owe them, settled (partyFilters.ts). For a customer, "you owe them" means they paid in advance, so it is labelled "Paid you in advance". Other liabilities don't exist in this filter, so none are invented.
Disagree
"A one-line day sentence is too big." I agree it was oversized and vague, so it's gone. But I've kept the four figures as one ruled line rather than return to cards, because each figure needs its basis written under it. The line is the register's header, not a row of tiles.
Open
Settling a person who is both customer and supplier. You decided balances stay separate until explicitly settled (D-03). How a settlement is recorded (one linked pair of entries, or a new document) is not decided. The settle screen below is a proposal for you to approve, not a rule I'm assuming.

Step 7 · Destructive and money-moving actions

Cancel, return, refund and settle are separate

A bill's ⋯ menu first asks what happened, because each answer does something different to stock and money. The cancel dialog only lists consequences the server has checked for this bill, using the rules in sales/services/void.py.

Date picker and item search

Date picker

The existing UbDateInput, with the quick choices the payment form already passes (Today, Yesterday). Today has an outline, the chosen date is filled, keyboard focus has a ring, and days the field doesn't allow are faded. In this example the field doesn't allow future dates.

Picking a day sets it and closes the picker; closing without picking keeps the old date. There's no separate Apply step, which is how the component works today. Keyboard movement inside the calendar (arrows by day, Page Up and Page Down by month) is what this design asks for; it needs checking in UbDateInput before being promised.

The existing UbAsyncCombobox with useItemSearch, at the real width of the bill's item cell. Each option says its usual price, the unit and whether GST is included, and its stock in words. What would move it to 9

  • Fix TV-01 (no netting) and TV-02 (Shop reminders) on the server, so the lanes show real separate figures.
  • Decide how a dual-role settlement is recorded.
  • Run 5 to 8 shopkeepers through timed tasks: take ₹2,000 from Mohan across three bills, find who is most late, cancel a wrong bill, share a receipt in Hindi.
  • Check on real Android and iPhone devices: keyboard-open footers, large system text at 320 px, slow 3G.
  • Run the existing axe sweep and a screen-reader pass on the built screens.

Still needs interactive or real-world testing

  • Whether the find-or-start box is discovered and trusted, or ignored.
  • Whether "Repeat" suggestions are useful or noise for shops with irregular customers.
  • Double taps on Receive on a slow network, and recovery after closing the app mid-save.
  • A payment recorded on another device while the form is open.
  • Hindi readability of the day line and lane labels at 320 px with large text.
  • Staff roles: which figures each role should see, checked against existing permissions.
  • Busy shops with 200+ bills a day: register paging and the per-hour chart.
Appendix · Research

What we borrow, and what we leave behind

You asked for something that doesn't look like these apps. So we take a few working patterns from each and none of their look. Everything below comes from official help pages or published write-ups. I have not used these apps hands-on, and where a source is a third-party case study it says so.

myBillBook POSOfficial help article, accessed 17 Oct 2026Source
Pattern
"+ Hold & Create Another" parks a bill and opens a new one. Out-of-stock items are flagged in red as they are added.
Problem it solves
A second customer arrives while the first is still choosing.
We take
Parked bills appear in the register as a quiet "On hold · 2" row you can tap back into. This builds on the existing durable sales drafts.
We leave
The dense POS grid and a separate POS mode. A busy counter needs one billing screen, not two.

VyaparOfficial product pages and TaxOne shortcut help, accessed 17 Oct 2026BillingShortcuts
Pattern
Barcode adds items to a bill. Ctrl+S saves and Ctrl+M opens a transaction drawer. Bills and reminders go out on WhatsApp.
Problem it solves
Desktop counters run on the keyboard. Taking a hand to the mouse costs seconds on every bill.
We take
One shortcut opens the Counter Bar (Ctrl+K or /), and Ctrl+Enter saves, which the bill editor already supports.
We leave
"10-second invoicing" marketing claims and accounting vocabulary. Our users get plain words.

UbLineItemsEditor keys, useScannerListener.

The shortcut list is for Vyapar TaxOne, not the billing app.

Zoho InventoryOfficial dashboard help, accessed 17 Oct 2026Source
Pattern

Problem it solves
It shows what is stuck, not just what was sold.

The Follow-ups tab: open orders, challans not billed, purchase orders not received, promises due today. Each row says its next step.

Warehouse stages (pick, pack, ship) most kirana and hardware shops don't have, and a six-widget grid.

Zomato merchant appPranu Sarna, UX Planet, 28 Jan 2018 (third-party design write-up)Source
Pattern
Orders are sorted by urgency, not recency. They use high-contrast type and no heavy shadows or animation on cheap Android phones, and tap response went from about 280 ms to 170 ms.
Problem it solves
A busy person glancing at a screen from a distance must see what is due now.
We take
Follow-ups sorted by urgency, large tabular numbers, and a tap-response budget of 100 ms on a mid-range Android phone.
We leave
Countdown tickers. The same article reports they caused anxiety in testing.
Maps to
Sorting on the server, the existing motion tokens.
Limits
It is from 2018 and written by the designer, not an independent study.
