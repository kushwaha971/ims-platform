# Part 1 — Executive Summary

*If you read only one chapter of this specification, read this one. It states what UdhaarBook is, who it serves, why it can win, what we will build first, how it is architected, how it makes money, what could kill it, and what success looks like at six, twelve and twenty-four months. Everything asserted here is elaborated in Parts 2–23 and traceable to the three research dossiers cited inline as [R1] (Zoho Inventory), [R2] (Khatabook and Indian ledger apps) and [R3] (market and review research).*

## 1.1 What UdhaarBook is

UdhaarBook is a mobile-first, multi-tenant, white-labelable business operating system for Indian small businesses of every type. Its centre of gravity is the **party ledger** — the *udhaar khata* that almost every Indian business already keeps, on paper or in a free app — and everything else in the product exists to feed that ledger with truth: GST-compliant billing, inventory, purchases, payments and expenses. The product's single promise is *one book instead of four apps*: the shopkeeper who today runs a paper bahi-khata for credit, a WhatsApp thread for orders, an Excel sheet for stock and a visit to the CA with a shoebox of bills [R3 §4.4] gets one place where a sale simultaneously issues a bill, moves stock, posts a ledger entry, produces a UPI collection link and lands in the GST summary.

The formal definition is fixed in Part 0 §0.1 and is normative: *a mobile-first, white-label business operating system for Indian small businesses of every type, built around a party ledger (udhaar/khata) that is fed by GST-compliant billing, inventory and payments — one book of truth instead of four apps.* The vendor is Metis Labs; the first market is India (INR, GST, UPI, WhatsApp, Hindi and English), with tax regime, currency and locale kept tenant-configurable so that the architecture does not have to be rebuilt for a second country.

Three structural choices distinguish UdhaarBook from everything else in its category and are worth stating at the outset.

**The ledger is the spine, not a report.** In Zoho, Tally, Vyapar and myBillBook, receivables are a *consequence* of invoices — a derived view called "Customer Balance Summary" or "Receivable Ageing". Zoho Inventory in particular has no khata UX at all: "receivables are invoice-centric; no running party ledger with cash-in/cash-out entries without an invoice, no interest/late fee, no collection reminders by SMS at scale" [R1 §E.2]. In UdhaarBook the `LedgerEntry` is a first-class, immutable primitive that can be created directly ("you gave ₹500") *or* generated by a document. A business can use the product for six months with nothing but the ledger and still be using it correctly.

**Business type is a default-setter, not a fork.** One codebase serves a kirana store, a wholesale trader, a garment retailer, a repair service, a CA practice and a small job-worker. The onboarding business-type choice seeds defaults — whether inventory is on, which units appear, which document kinds are offered, whether parties are called "Customer" or "Party" — and nothing more (Part 0 §0.2). This is a direct response to Zoho's most-cited usability complaint: the product "doesn't allow removing unneeded features, leading to clutter" and "I see a lot of tools I don't really know how to use" [R1 §E.1 #3].

**White-label is a first-class delivery mode, not an afterthought.** Every large merchant-facing distribution owner in India — Paytm with Business Khata, HDFC with SmartHub Vyapar, PhonePe, BharatPe, Airtel with Bajaj Finance, Bizom for FMCG brands — has built or bought some SMB tool and stopped at the layer adjacent to its own revenue. None has built ledger + billing + inventory in one, and none is known to license it out [R3 §8.1, §8.2]. UdhaarBook is designed from day one so that a `Partner` record owns branding, entitlements and support for a set of tenants, making the product sellable both direct and through a bank, NBFC, distributor or ERP vendor.

## 1.2 The problem

India has roughly 7.83 crore Udyam-registered MSMEs and 1.67 crore active GST taxpayers [R3 §7.1]. Payments digitisation is finished — 24.5 billion UPI transactions a month, over 90% of surveyed MSMEs accepting digital payments — while record-keeping digitisation has barely started: only 22% of kiranas plan any technology investment, 46% of MSMEs are entirely offline, and 52.6% say that *finding the right digital tool* is itself a problem [R3 §7.2]. That gap between "everyone takes UPI" and "almost nobody keeps books" is the market.

Underneath it sits a specific, expensive, daily pain: **credit**. Udhaar is not an edge case in Indian commerce, it is the operating norm — Paytm's own product page observes that "udhaar is a very common term in business" and targets kiranas, laundries and newspaper vendors [R3 §4.4]. A shopkeeper who extends credit faces three problems at once. He does not reliably know who owes him what. He finds it socially awkward to ask for money. And when money does arrive over UPI, nothing connects that credit to the name in his notebook, so the notebook drifts out of truth. The single loudest complaint across every Indian ledger app is exactly this last failure — "money debited, ledger not updated" [R2 §C.2, §D #2].

The tools available to him all solve one slice and force him to assemble the rest:

- **Free khata apps (Khatabook, OkCredit)** solve the ledger beautifully and stop there. They have no real inventory, no GST depth, thin reporting, and no third-party integrations [R3 §2.2]. When the business grows past a notebook, the app cannot grow with it.
- **Paid billing apps (Vyapar, myBillBook, Swipe)** solve GST billing and stock, but treat udhaar as "receivables", are English/Hindi-only in the billing half, and are criticised for data-integrity failures, support and renewal price rises [R3 §2.2, §2.3].
- **Desktop incumbents (TallyPrime, Busy, Marg)** solve compliance depth and are trusted by the CA, but their number-one review gap is the absence of mobile and cloud — "missing mobile app" is TallyPrime's most-mentioned G2 con [R3 §2.2, §2.3].
- **Zoho** solves breadth superbly but is priced and shaped for a different buyer: ₹999–₹7,499/month for Inventory alone, with serial/batch tracking gated at ₹2,299/month, GST returns living in a *separate* product (Zoho Books) that must be run on the same org, and no khata UX at all [R1 §Part B, §E.2].

The result is the "multi-app trap": ledger in one app, billing in another, stock in a spreadsheet, compliance at the CA, and four sets of numbers that never quite agree.

## 1.3 Who it is for

UdhaarBook serves owner-operated and staffed small businesses of all types. The research supports a deliberate two-population strategy rather than a single ICP.

The **acquisition population** is the micro business: the kirana, the tailor, the mobile-repair shop, the vegetable wholesaler, the electrician. Owner plus zero to two helpers, ₹10–50 lakh turnover, often below the GST threshold or under composition, operating on an Android phone. This population is enormous (12–20 million kiranas alone [R3 §7.1]) and has a demonstrated willingness to *adopt* — Khatabook reached 50M+ installs and 10M monthly actives [R2 §A.0] — but a near-zero willingness to *pay*.

The **revenue population** is the GST-registered, staffed business: the garment or hardware or electronics retailer with two to ten staff and ₹40 lakh–₹5 crore turnover; the wholesaler or distributor with godowns and salesmen; the professional services firm; the small manufacturer or job-worker. These businesses buy software today, at ₹3,400–₹4,000 a year for single-user mobile-plus-desktop and ₹8,000–₹25,000 for multi-user [R3 §3.1]. They pay because compliance forces them to and because staff need to be given a system.

The research is unambiguous that these are different populations and that confusing them is fatal: *"Free khata apps with 10M+ MAU could not get kiranas to pay; paid billing+inventory apps do get paid, at ₹3.4–4k/yr, at ~2% conversion. The unit of monetisation is the GST-registered, staffed business with a PC, not the kirana with a phone"* [R3 #1, High confidence]. UdhaarBook therefore designs its free tier to acquire the micro business and its paid tiers around what the staffed business actually needs: multiple devices, staff with roles, inventory depth, GST outputs and the CA hand-off.

The third persona that appears in every plan is the **accountant**. The recurring "1 user + 1 CA" structure at myBillBook and Zoho Books Free shows that the CA is a second, non-paying seat in almost every Indian SMB [R3 §4.2]. UdhaarBook includes an `accountant` role with read-and-export-everything permissions, free, in every plan.

## 1.4 Why now

Four enabling conditions have arrived simultaneously and none of them existed when Tally defined this market.

*Distribution is solved.* UPI runs at 24.5 billion monthly transactions and WhatsApp has 535 million Indian users with 15 million active Business accounts [R3 §7.1]. A product that shares over WhatsApp and collects over UPI needs no distribution technology of its own.

*Compliance is a forcing function on a schedule.* GST 2.0 restructured the slabs to 0/5/18/40 plus 3% and 0.25% with effect from 22 September 2025, abolishing 12% and 28% [R2 §C.1]. E-invoicing is mandatory above ₹5 crore aggregate turnover with a 30-day reporting window at ₹10 crore [R2 §C.1]; 8.56 lakh GSTINs already generate IRNs [R3 §7.1]. Every one of these events forces a software decision on a business that had been managing on paper.

*The incumbents have a structural, admitted gap.* Tally's top-mentioned weakness is mobile; Busy's and Marg's reviewers ask for cloud; Marg's support sub-score is 2.9 out of 5 [R3 §2.1, §2.3]. Meanwhile the mobile-first apps' admitted weaknesses are depth, multi-user, offline reliability and Tally export. *"The open middle is mobile-first and desktop-capable, multi-user with roles, offline-tolerant, Tally-exportable. Both ends of the market request exactly this"* [R3 #3, High].

*Data protection is about to become a design constraint rather than a differentiator.* The DPDP Act 2023 with Rules notified 13 November 2025 puts substantive obligations — notice, consent, erasure, breach reporting, grievance redress — in force at Phase 3 on 14 May 2027, with MeitY floating an earlier date [R2 §C.6]. A product designed now can bake consent, purpose limitation and erasure into its data model at near-zero cost; a product retrofitted in 2027 cannot.

## 1.5 Why UdhaarBook is different

Against **Zoho**, the differentiation is *shape and price*. Zoho is a superb, deep, document-centric inventory and accounting suite that costs ₹999–₹7,499 per month per organisation, gates batch and serial tracking (essential to any pharma or FMCG kirana) behind a ₹2,299/month tier, meters growth by counting sales orders *and* invoices *and* purchase orders *and* bills separately, ships an English-only Indian UI, has no native Tally export, and does not contain GST returns at all — those live in Zoho Books [R1 §Part B, §E.1 #1, §E.2]. UdhaarBook prices by users and outlets rather than documents, puts batch/expiry and barcode inside the base plan rather than a premium tier, ships Hindi alongside English at MVP, and treats the CA hand-off as a first-class output.

Against **Khatabook**, the differentiation is *depth and durability*. Khatabook proved the demand for a digital khata at extraordinary scale, but it never built the business past the ledger: inventory and GST billing are a separately-licensed reseller SKU, reporting is thin, reminders route through external apps, and the monetisation thesis migrated to lending, which generated ₹102.7 crore of FY24 revenue against ₹116 crore of losses and which dominates its negative reviews [R2 §A.0, §A.1 F20; R3 §2.2]. UdhaarBook keeps Khatabook's ledger ergonomics exactly — red/green, two big buttons, one-tap entry, vernacular copy — and then lets the same business issue a GST invoice, run stock, and hand a register to its CA without changing apps or paying a reseller.

Against **Vyapar and myBillBook**, the differentiation is *trust and language*. Their most damaging complaints are not about missing features: they are "calculation errors", "missing data, mismatched totals at audit", crashes and resets after updates, and price rises [R3 §2.2, §2.3]. UdhaarBook's answer is architectural rather than promotional — immutable ledger entries and stock movements with corrections posted as reversals plus replacements, `transaction.atomic()` around every state change, an append-only audit log with before/after values, and server-computed totals with the client's arithmetic treated as a preview (Part 0 §0.11). Language is the second axis: vernacular support exists in the ledger half of the market and is missing from the billing half [R2 §B.2].

Against **Tally**, the differentiation is *who it is for*. Tally is software for the person who keeps the books. UdhaarBook is software for the person who runs the shop, with an export for the person who keeps the books.

## 1.6 The MVP thesis

**The MVP is: everything a business needs to throw away the paper khata, the bill book and the stock register on day one — and nothing else.** That resolves to 67 features across platform, parties, ledger, inventory, sales, purchases, payments, expenses, reports, notifications and import/export (Part 16 §16.15).

Concretely, at MVP a business can: sign up with a mobile number and OTP and create one or several businesses; add parties who may be customer, supplier or both, with opening balances imported from CSV; record "you gave" and "you got" entries with notes, photos, backdating and immutable corrections; see a running balance, aging buckets and a branded PDF statement shareable over WhatsApp; set a collection date per party and fire manual or (where a provider is configured) automated D-1/D0 reminders; maintain items with HSN/SAC, GST slab, prices, MRP, reorder point and opening stock; issue estimates, tax invoices and bills of supply that are Rule 46-compliant, numbered per financial year, printed to A4 or 80mm thermal, and carrying a UPI QR; post credit notes and voids that reverse both stock and ledger; record purchase bills and supplier payments; take payments in multiple modes with split tender and allocation; log expenses and read a cashbook; and run a dashboard, day book, sales and purchase registers, aging, stock summary and a GST summary, all exportable.

What is deliberately **not** in the MVP: multi-location and transfers, variants, price lists, batches and expiry, barcode scanning, sales orders, e-invoice and e-way bill, WhatsApp Business API templates, payment-aggregator integration, an online store, lending, and a double-entry general ledger. Each exclusion has a reason recorded in Parts 2 and 7; the most important is the sequencing lesson from Zoho's own ten-year history, where the *paper trail* shipped in year one and physical-warehouse constructs — bins, picklists, putaways, stock counts — arrived in years nine and ten [R1 §C.3 lesson 1]. Online storefronts are excluded on stronger evidence still: Khatabook's MyStore, OkCredit's OkShop and Dukaan's kirana product all shut [R3 #13, High].

## 1.7 The phased plan

**MVP (74 features)** delivers parity with the free khata apps plus the billing and stock capability they lack, for a single location, single currency, GST-India tenant. Success is a real business running its whole month on it.

**Phase 2 (41 features)** adds the depth that staffed and wholesale businesses need and that partners require: multi-location with transfers, variants, price lists, secondary units, barcode scan and label printing, delivery challans, recurring invoices, purchase orders and GRN, debit notes, landed cost, the Razorpay payment-aggregator adapter with an unmatched-payments queue, WhatsApp Business API utility templates, web push, the in-app help centre and tours, partner domains and a partner admin console, contact import, party merge and the public "view your khata" link.

**Phase 3 (24 features)** is compliance automation and verticals: e-invoice (IRN) and e-way bill through a GSP adapter, GSTR-1 JSON and Tally XML export, batches and expiry, stock take, serial numbers, sales orders, interest and late fees, collection routes and salesman collections, custom roles, tenant API keys and webhooks, bank statement import, cheque tracking and the optional loans module.

**Future** holds composite items and BOM, marketplace sync, and a full double-entry general ledger with P&L and balance sheet — explicitly out of scope for MVP architecture beyond the extension points named in Part 20.

## 1.8 The architecture in one paragraph

UdhaarBook is a Django 5.2 modular monolith on PostgreSQL 16 behind a Next.js App Router frontend, deployed by docker-compose on a single VPS and scalable to Kubernetes only when a partner requires it. Every business row carries a `tenant_id` and passes through a fail-closed tenant-scoped manager; cross-tenant identifiers return 404, never 403. Primary keys are time-ordered UUIDv7; human-facing numbers come from per-tenant, per-financial-year sequences. Money and quantity are `Decimal` end to end — `numeric(14,2)` for money, `numeric(14,3)` for quantities — and cross the API as strings. Ledger entries and stock movements are immutable; corrections post a reversal plus a replacement linked by `supersedes_id`, and every state-changing endpoint is atomic, idempotency-keyed and audit-logged. The frontend is Redux Toolkit with per-feature slices and thunks over Axios, React Hook Form with Yup schemas, TanStack Table inside a `UbDataGrid`, `react-intl` for `en` and `hi`, and Tailwind plus `ml-uikit` primitives wrapped as `Ub*` components — no MUI, no TanStack Query, no Celery and no Redis at MVP, with scheduled work running as idempotent management commands behind a `jobs` abstraction that Celery can replace later without touching callers. PDFs render client-side through the browser's print path at MVP. SMS, WhatsApp and payments are adapter interfaces with console and deep-link implementations, so that real providers are configuration rather than surgery. The complete set of decisions and their ADR numbers is Part 0 §0.4; the schema is Part 21; the API is Part 22.

## 1.9 The commercial shape

Pricing follows the Indian market's proven structure rather than the global SaaS one. Indian SMB products are overwhelmingly **flat per business or per device, with user counts as tier steps**; per-user pricing draws tier-gating complaints, and renewal price rises are a top-three churn trigger [R3 #6, High; §3.3]. The ceiling for a single-user micro-business product is ₹0–₹4,000 a year; the staffed band runs ₹8,000–₹25,000 [R3 §3.1].

UdhaarBook therefore offers a **permanently free ledger tier** — parties, entries, statements, reminders, UPI QR, one user, one business, no ads, no entry caps — because ads are the most hated pattern in the category [R2 §D #1] and because capping transactions in a ledger destroys the very habit the product is trying to create. Revenue comes from a **paid tier priced per business per year with user-count steps**, whose wall is drawn exactly where the research says willingness to pay appears: multiple devices and users with roles, inventory and GST billing depth, server-sent messaging, exports and the CA hand-off. The CA seat is free in every tier. Prices are published, renewals are grandfathered, and multi-year options exist — three direct responses to the churn triggers in [R3 §3.3].

The second revenue line is the **partner channel**. Reseller distribution is the proven Indian SMB GTM: Tally has 28,000 partners, Vyapar 27,000 with top partners earning ₹2 lakh a month [R3 §8.1, §9 #9]. For white-label, the unit of pricing is the *active merchant*, not the seat, and support ownership is contractual — partner at L1, Metis at L2 — because support is simultaneously the biggest failure mode and the biggest cost in this market [R3 #9, High].

Lending is explicitly **not** a monetisation thesis. Khatabook's lending pivot produced revenue and losses together and dominates its negative reviews; OkCredit reached profitability only after *abandoning* lending and charging ₹30–99 a month to 200,000-plus paying shopkeepers [R2 §A.0, §B.4]. If a lending module is ever added it is opt-in, isolated from the ledger UX, and never a gate on a ledger feature.

## 1.10 The top five risks

**1. The free tier does not convert.** This is the category's defining failure: 10 million monthly actives produced almost no software revenue for Khatabook, and Vyapar's ~2% conversion on 10 million registrations yielded ₹69 crore against a ₹63 crore loss [R3 #1, §3.4]. *Mitigation:* design the paid wall around the staffed business from day one rather than discovering it later; treat the free tier as a marketing cost with a hard per-tenant infrastructure budget; make the partner channel a parallel revenue path that does not depend on self-serve conversion.

**2. Support becomes the product.** Support is the lowest sub-score for every Indian product in the set, and Marg's 3.2-star average is a support and channel failure rather than a product one [R3 §2.1, #2]. *Mitigation:* in-product self-service (help centre, contextual help, four guided tours) as a Phase 2 commitment; WhatsApp support in regional languages; contractual L1/L2 split with partners; published status page.

**3. Data-integrity failure destroys trust irrecoverably.** "Cost me thousands" and audit mismatches are the complaints that cause an accountant to advise switching [R3 #11, §3.3]. *Mitigation:* the immutability and atomicity rules in Part 0 §0.11 are non-negotiable engineering constraints, not preferences; reconciliation reports and CA-shareable audit exports ship at MVP.

**4. Compliance drift.** India generated GST (2017), e-invoicing (2020), repeated e-way-bill iterations, a slab restructure (Sep 2025) and new TDS/TCS sections (2026) in under a decade [R1 §C.3 lesson 6]. Hard-coding any threshold is a defect. *Mitigation:* GST slabs carry effective dates and rate history; every threshold named in [R2 Appendix] is a configuration row, not a constant.

**5. Scope gravity.** Zoho's own history shows a product that re-models core entities twice in five years and spends a third of its release notes on report columns [R1 §C.3 lessons 5, 7]. A small team building 67 MVP features can lose a year to breadth. *Mitigation:* the feature catalogue is the contract; the phase column is binding; anything outside the dependency policy needs an ADR (Part 0 §0.4, ADR-021).

## 1.11 What success looks like

The following are the targets against which this specification should be judged. They are deliberately concrete, and deliberately modest relative to the category's headline install numbers, because installs are not the metric that failed the incumbents — revenue and retention are.

### At 6 months (MVP shipped and in the field)

| Dimension | Target |
|---|---|
| Product | All 74 MVP features shipped, with FRD acceptance criteria met, `en` and `hi` strings complete |
| Adoption | 500 tenants created; 150 tenants active in the trailing 30 days (≥8 ledger entries or ≥4 documents in the month) |
| Depth of use | ≥40% of active tenants have both a ledger entry and a sales document in the same month — the proof that the "one book" thesis holds |
| Revenue | 25 paying tenants (self-serve or partner-billed); ₹2–3 lakh ARR — enough to prove that the paid wall is in the right place, not enough to matter |
| Partner | 1 signed white-label pilot with ≥50 merchants onboarded |
| Reliability | 99.5% monthly API availability; crash-free session rate ≥99.5%; zero unexplained balance discrepancies reported |
| Trust | Median time from "record you gave" tap to persisted entry < 400 ms on a 2 GB Android device |

### At 12 months

| Dimension | Target |
|---|---|
| Product | Phase 2 shipped or substantially shipped (multi-location, barcode, PO/GRN, payment aggregator, WhatsApp API, help centre) |
| Adoption | 5,000 tenants created; 1,500 monthly active tenants |
| Conversion | 5% free-to-paid on self-serve — 2.5× the ~2% category benchmark derived in [R3 §3.4] — justified by the paid wall sitting at multi-user/desktop rather than at transaction caps |
| Revenue | 300 paying tenants; ₹35–45 lakh ARR at a blended ₹12,000–₹15,000 per paying tenant across direct and partner |
| Partner | 3 partners live; ≥1,000 partner-channel merchants onboarded; partner-sourced revenue ≥30% of total |
| Retention | ≥80% 12-month logo retention among paying tenants; ≤5% monthly churn on the paid base |
| Support | Median first response < 4 working hours; ≥60% of help sessions self-served (help-centre view without a ticket) |

### At 24 months

| Dimension | Target |
|---|---|
| Product | Phase 3 compliance stack live: e-invoice (IRN), e-way bill, GSTR-1 JSON, Tally XML; batches/expiry and stock take for pharma/FMCG verticals |
| Adoption | 30,000 tenants created; 10,000 monthly active tenants |
| Revenue | 2,000–2,500 paying tenants; ₹3–3.5 crore ARR |
| Unit economics | Gross margin ≥75%; CAC payback < 12 months on the direct channel and < 6 months on the partner channel; **spend-per-rupee-of-revenue below 1.5**, against Vyapar's ₹2.04 at a comparable stage [R3 §3.4] |
| Partner | 6–8 partners live; ≥10,000 partner-channel merchants; a signed bank or NBFC partner using ledger data for underwriting |
| Market position | Present in Techjockey/SoftwareSuggest comparison sets for "GST billing software" and "khata app"; ≥4.5★ Play rating on ≥2,000 ratings with support as a *strength* in review text, inverting the category norm [R3 #2] |
| Compliance | DPDP Phase-3 readiness demonstrated ahead of 14 May 2027: consent records, erasure pipeline, breach runbook, grievance officer published [R2 §C.6] |

The single number that matters most across all three horizons is not installs and not ARR: it is the **share of active tenants using both the ledger and at least one document module in the same month**. That is the measurable form of the product thesis. If it is high, UdhaarBook has genuinely collapsed the multi-app trap and has a defensible position between Khatabook and Zoho. If it is low, the product is a khata app with extra screens, and the category's history says that does not monetise.
