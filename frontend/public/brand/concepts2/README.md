# YourKhata logo concepts, round 2

These are six new directions, drawn after the owner asked for "something else" than concepts A, B and C in `../concepts/`. None of them is applied to the app, and nothing here is committed. The owner will choose.

Files for each concept:

- `concept-N-mark.svg` is the 64 × 64 tile.
- `concept-N-lockup.svg` is the tile plus the "YourKhata" wordmark. The wordmark is DM Sans outlined to paths, so it needs no font.
- `concept-4-mark-16.svg` and `concept-6-mark-16.svg` are simplified 16 px cuts, pixel-aligned for the favicon.

Preview sheets:

- `/tmp/e2e-shots/brand/concepts2.png` shows each mark at 128, 48, 32 and 16 px, in one colour, as a lockup, on a dark surface, and as a home-screen icon.
- `/tmp/e2e-shots/brand/concepts2-in-context.png` shows each mark in the desktop rail, the mobile top bar and the drawer, and as a 16 px favicon.

Every mark works in one colour: a black tile with white figures, and the gaps stay tile-coloured. Only concepts 1 and 2 contain letterforms.

All contrast figures are WCAG 2.x ratios. Wherever text sits on colour, it passes 4.5:1. The only failure is white on the saffron tile, and that tile carries no text.

---

## 1. Signature K (indigo)

The lettering is custom. The K's leg keeps going below the baseline and sweeps under "hata" as a ruled line. A shopkeeper underlines the total and signs the bill, and here the name draws that line itself, so the ledger idea lives in the type instead of a picture of a book. The mark is the K with its own short rule.

| Role | Hex | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Tile, and the K in the wordmark | `#4A47D6` | White on tile 6.6:1; K on white 6.6:1 |
| Deep | `#2E2B93` | |
| Ink (wordmark) | `#0A090B` | 19.9:1 on white |

- **Strengths:** it keeps the current indigo and the DM Sans voice. It is the most "brand name first" option, and it is fully legible at 16 px.
- **Risks:** on its own the mark can read as "K_". A letterform also contradicts a white-label tenant's name.

## 2. Joined YK (deep teal)

This is a monogram. The Y's right arm runs straight up into the K's stem, so "Your" and "Khata" are one unbroken stroke: the customer and the shop share one account. The flat-cut tops and feet give it the look of a mohar (rubber stamp). The K's leg is mint, but it is decorative and goes white in one colour.

| Role | Hex | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Tile | `#0B6763` | White on tile 6.7:1; teal on white 6.7:1 |
| Mint (K leg) | `#8FE0D2` | Decorative |
| Deep | `#06413F` | |
| Ink | `#0B1F1E` | 17.1:1 on white |

- **Strengths:** it is the most ownable and trademark-able of the six, it reads "YK" down to 16 px, and teal stands apart from blue fintech.
- **Risks:** it contains letters, which breaks the rule in `UbLogo` that the mark is not a letterform so that white-label tenants stay safe. Teal is also close to a green that is common in this category, so check it against competitors before adopting.

## 3. Hisaab Barabar (ledger green)

This one is abstract. One disc is cut into two equal halves that slide past each other. What was given (diya) equals what was taken (liya), and the account moves but always closes back into one circle. The two tones stand for the two parties.

| Role | Hex | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Tile | `#146C43` | White on tile 6.4:1; green on white 6.4:1 |
| Leaf tint (lower half) | `#B5E8C9` | Decorative |
| Deep | `#0B4429` | |
| Ink | `#0C1F15` | 17.2:1 on white |

- **Strengths:** it is the simplest silhouette and the clearest at 16 px. It has no letters and no rupee, so it is safe for white-label, and it can take any tenant's primary ramp. Green means "settled".
- **Risks:** offset half-discs are a known abstract motif, so it is less distinctive until the brand builds meaning into it.

## 4. Dukaan (saffron)

A striped, scalloped awning sits over a shop front with a window and a door. It is the neighbourhood shop, friendly and open. Saffron is warm, local and nothing like fintech blue. In the wordmark, "Khata" is set in burnt saffron.

| Role | Hex | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Tile | `#EF7A12` | White on tile **2.8:1, fails**: no white text on this tile. Brown ink on tile 6.2:1 |
| Burnt saffron ("Khata") | `#B34700` | 5.5:1 on white |
| Stripes and door | `#8A3403` | |
| Ink | `#2A1406` | 17.5:1 on white |

- **Strengths:** it is the warmest and most "for shops" option, and instantly recognisable as a home-screen icon.
- **Risks:** a shop-front icon is a common picture, including stock "store" icons, so it is less ownable. It needs its own 16 px cut. Any button or text in the brand colour must use `#B34700`, not the tile colour.

## 5. The Nudge (indigo and saffron)

This is a speech bubble with a squared "speaking" corner: a squircle whose tail sits flush with its side, not a circle, and with no phone in it. A saffron ping is notched into its corner. It stands for the polite payment reminder that gets the money back without an awkward call, which is the product's best moment.

| Role | Hex | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Tile | `#4A47D6` | White on tile 6.6:1 |
| Ping | `#FF9F1C` | Decorative; ink on it 9.7:1 |
| Deep | `#2E2B93` | |
| Ink | `#0A090B` | 19.9:1 on white |

- **Strengths:** it tells the value proposition in one picture, keeps the indigo, and is clean at 16 px.
- **Risks:** a chat bubble with a badge is category-generic and reads as a messaging app. It says "reminders" more than "khata".

## 6. Tally (charcoal and marigold)

This is a bold geometric tally: four white bars struck through by a marigold fifth. It is the oldest way of keeping count, it is free of the rupee and of any language, and the struck bar says "counted, done".

| Role | Hex | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Tile and ink | `#1C1A24` | White on tile 17.2:1; ink on white 17.2:1 |
| Marigold | `#FFB020` | Charcoal on it 9.4:1 |

- **Strengths:** it is the boldest and most premium option, and it stands out on any home screen.
- **Risks:** it is busy at 16 px, so it needs its own three-bar cut. A near-black tile disappears on dark surfaces and would need a hairline there. Tally marks are also used by some counting and "habit" apps.

---

## Recommendation

**Concept 3, Hisaab Barabar**, with **concept 5, The Nudge**, as runner-up.

Concept 3 has the cleanest 16 px read. It carries the product's promise, "the account balances", and it is the only strong option that respects the existing white-label rule: the mark is not a letterform and is repainted from the tenant's primary ramp.

Choose concept 2 instead only if the owner wants a YK monogram enough to give up that rule.
